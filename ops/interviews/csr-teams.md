# Interview guide: Corporate CSR teams

> Draft by a free model through the FreeLLMAPI gateway, linted, then read and edited by Claude. Not yet reviewed by the founder and not yet used in a conversation.

## Who this is for
This guide is for founders interviewing corporate CSR decision-makers. It targets those who fund heritage, culture, or community projects in India.

## Before you start
- Listen more than you speak.
- Take their words down exactly.
- No names in notes.
- Keep it to 30 minutes.

## Opening
I am researching how heritage visitor experiences are currently run. I want to learn from your work in funding these projects. I am not pitching anything today.

## Questions
1. What does your role involve on a typical day?
2. Tell me about the last heritage, culture or community project your company funded. How did it first reach you?
3. What did you do between receiving it and saying yes or no?
4. What did it cost, and how long did the decision take?
5. Who else was involved in that decision?
6. How did you decide between it and other proposals?
7. What did you fund in this area before, and what happened to it?
8. What got in the way of the last project you funded or turned down?
9. How do you find out whether a funded project worked?
10. How does a new partner or project get approved?
11. Who must say yes to the budget?
12. What did you wish had been different about the last proposal you reviewed?

## Commitments to ask for
- A follow-up conversation with a date.
- An introduction to the person who decides.
- Sight of a real document such as a budget line, tender, or proposal template.
- A small paid or signed pilot.
- Say to ask for at least one before the call ends.

## Coding the answers
| Tier | Meaning | Example from this segment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| T0 | Opinion | "This is a big problem." |
| T1 | Stated intent | "We plan to fix this." |
| T2 | Past behaviour | "We hired local guides last year." |
| T3 | Time or reputation commitment | "I will send you the contact." |
| T4 | Money or signed pilot | "We signed the pilot agreement." |

## Do not
- Do not pitch a product or solution.
- Do not ask for opinions on hypotheticals.
- Do not ask about the future.
- Do not accept compliments as evidence.
